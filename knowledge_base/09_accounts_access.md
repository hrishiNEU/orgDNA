# Information Security: Phishing, MFA, and Protecting Your Account

Northeastern's Office of Information Security (https://security.its.northeastern.edu/) protects university data and your online identity. Everyone with a .edu email address is a high-value target for credential theft.

## Phishing: how to spot it and what to do
- **Northeastern will NEVER ask you to confirm your username and password in an email.** Any message that does is phishing.
- **Report suspicious email:** use the **Report Message button** in your Outlook client to report it immediately.
- **Think your account is compromised?** Email **phishcatcher@northeastern.edu** right away to report it to the Office of Information Security, and change your password.
- Watch for urgency ("your account will be closed today"), fake job offers, and login pages that aren't northeastern.edu addresses.

## Multi-factor authentication (MFA)
MFA is required to access many university systems (email, Student Hub, VPN, and more). It adds a second verification step — typically an approval on your phone — so a stolen password alone isn't enough. **Duo** is the most common MFA service at the university. Enroll and manage your devices through your Northeastern account settings; keep a backup method registered in case you replace your phone.

## Good security habits
- Never reuse your Northeastern password on other sites
- Keep your OS and browser updated; Northeastern supports Chrome, Edge, Safari, and Firefox
- Follow the Appropriate Use of Computer and Network Resources Policy (northeastern.edu/aup)
- Book a free security consultation or take security awareness training through the Information Security site — every October, Cybersecurity Awareness Month offers extra events and resources
